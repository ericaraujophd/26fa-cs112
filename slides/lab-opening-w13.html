Who she was

Marcella of Rome

Rome · c. 325 – 410

  • A Roman noblewoman of a consular family, widowed seven months into her marriage.
  • A rich old consul proposed. She said she would look for a husband, not an inheritance — and when he argued that old men sometimes outlive young ones, she replied that “a young man may indeed die early, but an old man cannot live long.”
  • She was the first highborn woman in Rome to publicly profess the monastic life, when the word was still a term of reproach.
  • Her house on the Aventine became a school. Paula and Eustochium were formed there.
  • After Jerome left Rome, disputed questions about Scripture were referred to her to settle.
  • In the sack of Rome in 410 soldiers beat her for gold she did not have. She begged them only to spare her student Principia, and died a few days later.

Today she is the one who would not take an answer on authority alone.
